# Novelty and evidence boundary

JointVerify proposes an **OpenRSI improvement task for learning how to allocate executable public integration checks and repair across two frozen coding workers, under shared inference and test budgets, with evaluation on different repositories**. The submission is a reusable policy, not manually solved feature patches. The question is whether dependency changes and stale test evidence identify checks worth their cost beyond competent fixed schedules and serialization.

This is a bounded task-level distinction. **Adaptive verification, learned multi-agent coordination and budget-aware collaboration already have substantial prior art.** We do not claim a new underlying dataset, the first multi-agent coding system, or that no related method exists anywhere. The inspected public OpenRSI catalog contained no equivalent submitted task; that finding covers the saved 2026-09-23 snapshot of 42 Task Ideas, 337 top-level comments, two replies and 67 PR records, not private work or later submissions. Searches for CooperBench, Claim Plane, JointVerify and joint verification returned no matches in that snapshot.

The remaining empirical question is whether revision/dependency evidence improves the allocation of scarce executable checks and repair on interacting feature pairs, and whether that policy transfers to different repositories. The inspected papers do not report this exact comparison; this is an observation about their experiments, not proof of a globally unstudied problem. Changed datasets, budgets or wrappers alone do not establish a new method. Revision invalidation is an engineering safeguard, and any claimed benefit must come from measured policy comparisons.

The strongest simple alternative may win: always integrate/test, serialize work, or retain the current team protocol. Those outcomes would falsify the proposed adaptive advantage. Ordinary symbolic analysis and recomputation remain allowed. A convincing study should also include a per-subtask executable-verification/repair control and a budget-aware collaboration-module selector, informed by the closer work above. Until their algorithms and original settings are reproduced, these must be labeled adaptations, not faithful VeriMAP or FutureWeaver reproductions.

## What published scores support

The official [team trajectory card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/CooperBench/team-trajectories) reports **403/651 = 61.9%** for team without typed protocol, 390/636 = 61.3% for full team, and 362/652 = 55.5% for solo, using its `gpt-5.5-hao` deployment and Codex harness. Different completed denominators prevent an unqualified paired comparison. These figures show both strong performance and remaining failures; they do not prove our policy improves anything.

The older [CooperBench leaderboard](https://cooperbench.com/leaderboard) reports **13.3% cooperative versus 21.6% solo** for Qwen3-Coder-30B under its older OpenHands/no-Git setup. This is motivation for testing a reproducible worker, **not a baseline for the current team, JointVerify's wrapper, our selected pairs or our budget**. We have no measured frozen-Qwen score for that new contract. Do not present old low scores as a current frontier ceiling or select a weaker worker to manufacture difficulty.

## What this code adds—and current limits

The local contribution supplies [isolated workers and explicit patch/message exchange](runner/worker.py), an [episode controller](runner/episode.py), [periodic/unconditional/dependency-heuristic schedules](policies/README.md), shared budget accounting, and separate public-check/final-feature grading paths. Check evidence is associated with candidate revisions, so an old pass does not certify a changed patch. The [study manifest](manifests/STUDY.md) fixes repository separation before policy tuning; the [compute lane](compute/README.md) pins a reproducible worker/runtime and prepares strict token accounting.

These are implementation assets for the research question, not evidence of a novel winning algorithm. The initial dependency detector is a limited regex heuristic, not complete semantic analysis. Infrastructure and oracle controls validate the three-case pilot; they do not measure coordination gains or validate all study cases. The local Codex pilot is a narrower action proxy and does not establish an immutable-worker or token-matched result.

**The full scientific control matrix still requires model execution and a faithful official-team implementation:** faithful current team without typed protocol, unconditional joint verification with repair, equal-budget always-serial, strong centralized solo, and the closer verification/planning adaptations described above. The prototype now implements periodic, unconditional, serial and centralized controls with the same tool/budget gateway; these do not constitute a faithful run of the complete official upstream team harness. Frozen-Qwen deployment, matched comparisons and repository-transfer outcomes also remain pending. Upstream public assets and repeated aggregate Judge feedback leave contamination/adaptive-overfitting limitations; neither global secrecy nor an untouched final holdout is claimed.
